truth, in one direction.

**Model:** Opus · high effort — this is where two large surfaces meet, and where the
restyle-everything trap is finally resolved deliberately instead of by accident.
**Size:** 1.5 days. **Depends on:** MB1, MB5, MB6, MB12. **Runs last.**

## Delivers

**The contract, written down first.** Before any code: one document naming every field the 3D Plan
reads from the mood board, who writes it, and what breaks if the shape changes. Extend
`03-REFERENCE/contracts.md` from the docs pack rather than starting a new list.

1. **Palette styles reach the room.** After MB5 the board holds a *style* (Our colours only /
   Softer room, richer people / Room and people) and a six-rank visibility hierarchy — not just a
   flat list of colours. The room derives materials from the resolved board, so the same wedding
   renders differently under a different style, as it should.
2. **Inspiration photos reach their zones.** A couple who uploaded a wall design sees it beside the
   3D wall they are dressing. Reference, not render — the photo sits next to the zone, never
   composited into it.
3. **Finalized parts are locked in the room too.** After MB12 a part the vendor agreed to build
   stops moving. It must stop moving *here as well*, with the same label saying who agreed and
   when. A part editable in one surface and frozen in the other is two mechanisms disagreeing about
   one fact — which is how both pass their own tests and still lie.
4. **The theme identity labels the room** — the couple's own theme name, not "Untitled event".
5. **Attire roles reach the attire-facing surfaces** consistently with what 02 now holds, including
   `custom_roles`.

## 🛑 The trap this session exists to resolve properly

MB1 forbids feeding the room's chair/floral slots from the palette editor's helper, because they
derive differently and reusing it **restyles every room already sold.** MB1 avoided the problem
by never touching it. **MB15 has to touch it — this is the whole point of the session.**

**Owner decision, 2026-09-04: auto-upgrade every room, existing and new, to the new
palette-style derivation. No opt-in, no "your room will look different" prompt.** The
recommendation was opt-in-with-warning, specifically because a couple who already showed a
booked supplier their room could see it change without notice — the owner heard that tradeoff
and chose auto-upgrade anyway. Build to that. Do not add an opt-in flag or a warning prompt;
they were considered and declined.

Because every room now DELIBERATELY changes, the multi-select-widening technique of "byte-compare
before and after, zero differences" no longer applies as a pass/fail gate — the new render is
*supposed* to differ. What replaces it: **characterise the change, don't hide from it.**
- Render every one of the 2,600 seeded configurations under both the old and new derivation and
  report the diff as data (how many change, in what way) so the blast radius is known and
  documented, not asserted blind.
- The invariant that still MUST hold exactly: a room's **majors and zones that have no
  style-derived counterpart** (anything MB1 already draws faithfully — the 5th colour, the three
  new decor zones) must be unaffected by this switch. Only the chair/floral/room-dressing
  derivation is changing; nothing else should move. Byte-compare THOSE specific outputs.

## One direction, always

The mood board is where colour and design are decided. **The 3D Plan reads; it never writes back.**
Same rule as 00 → 02, for the same reason: two writers for one fact is a defect that takes months
to surface and always surfaces in front of a customer.
