The sidebar — how it behaves

  • It never leaves. Owner-locked 2026‑08‑13 over the YouTube screenshots: “the sidebar should stay.” It is sticky; moving between rooms never returns you to a different shell.
  • It never grows a submenu. Owner-locked 2026‑07‑15: “solid menu with no submenus.” A room’s own screens appear inside the room. That rule is what makes five rows survivable against 100 screens — and it is why the rest need an index, not an expander.
  • Every row carries a number, and it is the room’s own number. The rail prints the same fact the room’s status line prints, read once. Two numbers for one fact is how the badge on Your Team became three different mechanisms — which is still an open question on your desk, and is not re-asked here.
  • One right column at a time. At 1280 the room pins its own status column on the right. When the app’s wide panel opens, that column yields — measured in overview_FINAL: panel 420px, flow 782px, never the ~310px two fixed right columns would leave.
  • Below 1024 the rail is not there and nothing replaces it — the five rows are the five tabs. The top bar stays at every width, because it carries the bell and the account panel, and the account panel carries the only Sign out. The vendor prototype’s first draft hid it on the phone and stranded every supplier with no way out.

The bottom bar — what is in it

  • The same five, in the same order, in all three moments. Today the phone swaps its whole roster three times a year: Now · Check‑in · Seats · Event Hub · Schedule on the day, Overview · Review · Event Hub · Galleries after. So Check‑in and Seats are tabs for one day and homeless for the other 364, and “Review” and “Galleries” appear as words the couple has never seen before, on the day they are most tired.
  • The locked template does not change. bottom-nav.tsx keeps its frosted pill, its travelling indicator, its press bloom, its four motion knobs. Only the roster passed to it changes, which is what that component was built to take.
  • Badges mean “someone is waiting for you”, not “here is a count.” Overview and Your Team can show a red number. Guests shows 81 in the rail and nothing on the bar — 81 is wayfinding, and a red dot you can never clear teaches you to stop looking.

The FAB — off, for the same reason the vendor’s came off

  • CustomerNavFab is “Add guest” → /guests/new. Guests is one tap away in the same bar, and the Guests room opens with an Add control. Owner 2026‑09‑22 on the vendor side: “delete fab” — because a floating button that jumps to a subset of the room you are standing in is a door into the room you are standing in.
  • It is also paying for itself in pixels. globals.css: html[data-sn-fab] nav[aria-label='Primary navigation'] { right: 78px } against a bare right:14px. On a 390px screen that is 298px of pill instead of 362px — the FAB and the fifth tab are spending the same 64px, and only one of them is navigation.
  • What to delete: the wrapper dashboard/[eventId]/_components/customer-nav-fab.tsx and its mount in dashboard/[eventId]/layout.tsx — and nothing else. The shared NavFab primitive and AdminNavFab stay untouched, exactly as the vendor removal left them. Leave a note at the mount site saying the absence is deliberate; the vendor layout has one, and without it the next reader restores it “for consistency”. apps/web/scripts/port-control-baseline.json lists CustomerNavFab and must be regenerated from the merged tree.
  • ⚠ Your current worktree has an uncommitted fix on nav-fab.tsx (the sn-vt-fab view-transition name, which stopped the circle vanishing for 320ms on every tab change). That fix is for the primitive, which admin still mounts — so it is worth keeping on its own, and removing the couple’s wrapper does not undo it.
