Note for the owner — not part of the page

What changed from the second draft

  • The cards are posters. The cover-plus-caption shape is gone. Each celebration is one 3:4 sheet, and the names, the date and the status are composed inside it. A poster is the whole link; there is no strip below it and no "Open →". Nothing above the Celebrations panel was touched.
  • The markers you could not find are now a sash. "Up next" and "Coming soon" run across the top-left corner of their posters as a white ribbon, 12px bold caps, set in the poster's own dark (gold-800 on the gold poster, wine on the wine poster). It is the first thing in reading order and it is the one mark the house adds to a couple's poster, so it is the same shape on every poster that carries one. Movie Night carries none, as you ruled.
  • Maria & Jose — gold sky, white moon. Gold cannot hold a letter, so the art makes room: a white disc nearly the width of the sheet holds the names stacked in serif, a gold-800 ampersand, a hairline, "Saturday", and the date. Not one letter sits on gold. The lower third is open gold with a white thread ornament, the way a poster leaves ground under its subject.
  • Indalecio & Claire — wine, capiz, botanical, monogram. The capiz panes fill the upper sheet and fade out where the words begin. Two botanical sprigs rise behind a giant white I&C, the dominant element. The names are set in white below, the ampersand in gold-100 (6.5 on wine), then "Friday" and the date. The themes are no longer chips; they became the art, and they are also printed as a credit line at the foot the way a film poster carries credits.
  • Movie Night — a letterpress playbill. House stock, a heavy 3px rule inside a 1px rule, diamond-and-line ornaments, "MOVIE / NIGHT" in bold serif caps across the sheet, a thick-thin bar, "Thursday", and the date. Ink on paper-2 (12.7). It is plain on purpose and it should look as finished as the other two, not as if something failed to load.
  • The date is set with intent. Each poster prints the weekday in tracked caps over the date in serif. The weekdays are derived from the calendar, not invented: 12 December 2026 is a Saturday, 18 December 2026 a Friday, 20 August 2026 a Thursday. The "in 80 days / in 86 days" countdown from the second draft is gone; a printed poster does not count down.
  • The type scales with the sheet. Every size inside a poster is in cqw, a hundredth of that poster's own width, with a px fallback declared first. So the three posters keep the same composition at 375 (one column, ~343px wide), 900 (two columns, ~370px) and 1500 (three columns, ~360px) without a script.
  • Removed: the "Cale-Ice" hallmark the second draft printed under the I&C medallion. It is not in this brief's data table, so I did not carry it. If it is real (a moodboard name), it belongs on the wine poster as a second credit line and is a one-line restore.

What I am not sure about

  • One sash for every status, or a different device per poster. Your direction allowed "a sash, a stamp, a rule, a corner mark". I chose one device for all statuses so a person learns it once and reads it across the wall, but it means the house's mark, not the couple's design, is the most consistent thing on the page. A stamp for "Coming soon" and a sash for "Up next" would read as two different things — which they are — at the cost of that consistency.
  • The sash is white on the gold poster. An ink sash with white text would be louder there and would tie gold + ink + white. I kept it white so every sash is the same object; the trade is that on gold it is a quieter contrast than on wine.
  • The monogram is the dominant element on the wine poster; the names are on the gold poster. I varied this on purpose so the two weddings are recognisable from across a room. If every poster should lead with the names, the I&C shrinks to a mark and the names take its place.
  • 3:4, not 2:3. A printed poster is usually 2:3 (24×36). At 2:3 three posters in a row at 1500 would each be ~540px tall; 3:4 keeps the row visible without scrolling and still reads as a portrait sheet. Easy to change: one aspect-ratio.
  • Movie Night has no "held" or "finished" mark. The brief says no label for a past celebration, so it carries none. A poster for a past event on a real wall usually does not either — but a reader cannot tell from the poster alone that it is over, only from the date.
  • The white moon is the house's drawing for the "default" theme. It is what a couple with an accent colour and no theme gets. That is a house-style decision worth writing down somewhere real (the theme catalogue), because it will be the most common poster on the site.
  • cqw units need a 2023-or-later browser. Every size has a px fallback declared first, so an older browser gets a fixed-size composition rather than a broken one. The shipped component would use the same units; the real app's browser floor decides whether that is fine.

Anything I think is wrong in the brief

  • "No card has a photograph — every poster is drawn by the house." True today, and the three drawings hold. But the first couple who uploads a photograph will get a photo behind white type, and the sash and date block need a defined place to sit on an unknown image. I would decide now that a photograph gets a fixed ink gradient band at the foot for the type, and the sash stays where it is — otherwise the poster system has a fourth, undesigned case.
  • The label column says Movie Night has "none — it is past". Agreed for the label, but the ordering rule "Up next → Coming soon → past" will not hold once a person has several past celebrations: they will need a direction (newest past first, presumably) and probably a year rule. The second draft's note already raised the year heading; it stands.
