Event Hub — the final plan

What is decided, what already ships, what is left to build and in what order. Measured on origin/main 4104a71 and the live catalogue today, not read from the documents that made the claims. Every line is SHIPS, BUILD or OWNER. Revised the same evening after three more prototypes; the revisions are folded in, not appended.

Scope

This stream is the Event Hub website. Nothing else.

Not Papic, not Live Studio, not Patiktok, and not the story maker. “Our Love Story” on the hub is a read-only section rendering events.love_story. “Make my Story” is a separate Papic product that builds a 30-second reel in the browser, download-only. Same word, two products — a task that names one is not a task on the other.

The rule

Free is the page we write. Pro is changing how it looks.

“There is free preloaded. PRO is to customize it.” — and, the same day: “preloaded is also basic editing.”

Fixing your own words and facts is free. The prefill is a guess, and a guess a couple cannot correct publishes our wrong words under their names. Changing how it looks and moves is Pro. That is the whole rule; it is not a feature list, and a feature list would only rot beside it.

The test for any new feature, one line: are they fixing what we wrote, or changing how it looks? Fixing is free. Changing is Pro.

Part one

What is decided

Owner rulings, each recorded with where it lives. Do not re-ask any of these.

Decided

One shell, two middles

Three columns: layers and a short Add list on the left (own photos, a line of text, theme ornaments — no sticker library); the live thing in the centre with a contextual toolbar above it; the panel on the right; Done along the bottom with the section name and “See it as a guest”. On a phone the middle pins to the top, the tools become a swipe strip, and the panels rise as bottom sheets from a thumb bar. The shell never changes. Only the middle does.

Measured against lib/invitation-widgets.ts: eleven of the sixteen widgets are filled in, not composed — greeting, event details, countdown, schedule, venue map, dress code, what to bring, special message, RSVP, QR card, camera cues. A schedule is a list of times; dragging times is worse than typing them. Those get a form in the middle: an outline, and the fields of the selected row under a Content tab beside Animate. Five are composed — our love story, photos you add, gallery, custom sections, and the hero (fixed composition, but its background media and type are the couple’s). Those get a canvas with selection handles. Animate is the same panel on both; nothing typed is second class.

Owner, 2026-09-23: “this style” — approved as a style; and “i also want the people to animate the other parts of the event hub. mostly on the details of the event.” Prototypes: prototypes/hero-canvas-editor-2026-09-23.html (approved), prototypes/story-canvas-editor-2026-09-23.html (a canvas in the middle), prototypes/details-section-editor-2026-09-23.html (a form in the middle).

Decided

The controller is the page itself, prefilled

Not a settings list. The couple sees their own page with every blank already filled from their event details, each guess carrying a Suggested mark that invites a correction. Advanced holds the visibility switches, folded away — the content decides what shows, and the switches are an escape hatch most couples never touch. The Pro bill does not exist on the page until a Pro switch is on.

Prototype: prototypes/event-hub-fill-in-the-blanks-2026-09-22.html. Owner, 2026-09-23: “we have prefilled so they have something to start with”.

Decided

Rails on

Canva’s gestures — click to select with eight handles, one contextual toolbar, snapping, one Animate at three scopes (Page · Element · Text) — but not its free placement. Composition is bounded by the section and sits on a grid that reflows. A photo’s crop is a 3×3 focal point, never a pixel offset. A story chapter is an arrangement from four — photo left, photo right, full-bleed, words only — and every one becomes a stack at 375px.

Why: the page is 375px on a phone and 1440px on a laptop. A pixel offset is a bug waiting for a guest, and the guest finding it is worse than the couple being slightly constrained. Owner, 2026-09-23: “rails on.”

Decided

Motion is one choice of four. Everything else is Fine tune.

The panel had grown into a column of decisions for somebody with no vocabulary for any of them. Rails belong on the controls as much as on the canvas. So the couple picks one — Still · Calm · Editorial · Cinematic — and each preset sets every underlying value to a combination we designed, so there is no way to pick a bad one. The controls still exist, behind Fine tune: How it runs · In · During · Out · Stagger · Duration · Delay. Each group defaults to Auto, which applies nothing, so the preset keeps deciding until somebody deliberately overrides one thing.

In is how a thing arrives; During is what it does while the guest reads; they compose. How it runs is Plays once (time-driven) or Follows the scroll (scrubbed — stop and it freezes, scroll back and it reverses; animation-timeline: view(), no script). A Play button replays the entrance with all editing furniture hidden.

Out exists. The earlier draft of this page said it did not, and that was wrong. A timed exit does fight the reader — a section flinging itself away while somebody is still reading it — and that part stands. A scrubbed out is a different thing: the guest drives it, it reverses, and it is the handoff that makes the page one journey rather than a stack of blocks. So Out exists only under Follows the scroll: Fade away · Lift away · Settle back · Stay put. It is per element and staggered, never the whole widget leaving as a slab.

Owner, 2026-09-23: “so the transition to the next widget, that’s why i asked if there is an out animate” and “we still want it to be simple enough that they could customize this.” Verified in the prototype: the four presets produce genuinely different motion, an override wins over its preset, and returning a group to Auto hands control back to the preset. prototypes/details-section-editor-2026-09-23.html.

Decided

Two registers, one page: what you read moves, what you do stays still

Things you read — the hero, the countdown, the details, the schedule, the venue, the dress code, the story — are editorial. They are composed and they move: In, During, Out, played or scrubbed.

Things you do — the camera, Live Studio, the RSVP, find-my-table, E-Gifts, the QR card — stay still and behave like an app. A camera that fades in is a camera that is briefly unusable, and a guest reaching for it on the day is not admiring the page; they are trying to take a photograph. Motion on a function is a delay wearing a costume.

“But must be presented properly” is half the ruling, and it is the half that gets dropped. Still does not mean plain, unstyled or second class. The functions carry the theme’s type, colour and spacing exactly like everything else — they are simply not in motion. The bar to hold: a guest scrolls out of a beautifully animated story straight into the camera, and the camera feels like the best-made thing on the page, not like the animation ran out.

In the editor the services appear in the rail marked “Not animated” rather than being silently absent, so nobody goes looking for a panel that does not exist.

The test is “read or do”, never “important or unimportant”. Owner, 2026-09-23: “we only animate the details, the functions of the event hub stay as an app. but must be presented properly.”

Build 1Pro

Per-section backgrounds

🛑 CORRECTED 2026-09-23 — BUILD 1 IS LARGELY SHIPPED, AND THIS SECTION’S COLUMN CLAIM IS FALSE.
The text below says “No column exists” and proposes a new JSONB. It already exists. HubSectionCanvas in lib/hub-canvas.ts already carries media, focal (1–9), during and zoom, stored in invitation_widgets.config_json — that file’s own docblock says the column ALREADY EXISTS. The editor picker (sections-panel.tsx) and the renderer (hub-canvas-frame.tsx) both ship.

🔑 The grep in this plan was right and its conclusion was backwards: no ALTER TABLE invitation_widgets adds a background because none is needed, not because one is missing. Writing a new column would create a second competing home for one fact — the trap CLAUDE.md rule 8 records.

What is actually left: teach the existing background field three kinds instead of one. kind: ‘photo’ | ‘snippet’ | ‘color’ — today media is a bare ref so a photo is all it can be. No migration, no exposure freeze, no Ugat node.

⚠ The fence: media is held to the public bucket by siteMediaServeRef because config_json is couple-writable. A snippet must pass the same allow-list; a color must not go through the ref path at all.

No column exists. invitation_widgets carries type, order, visibility, mode and the per-type payloads added since (special message, what to bring, our photos, our love story) — nothing named background. The only background column on events is std_background, which belongs to the Save-the-Date.

Shape: one JSONB per widget row — { kind: 'photo' | 'snippet' | 'color', ref, focal: 1–9, during }. A photo or a snippet, never a film; the focal point is the 3×3 choice from the editor. Runs the exposure freeze like every new column.

Re-measure: git grep -n "ALTER TABLE public.invitation_widgets" supabase/migrations — none adds a background.

Build 4Pro — arranging, not fixing

Custom sections on the main page

The owner asked whether a couple can “add a blank screen in between, to create content on the website as well”. Today they cannot, on the main page. What ships is the after-the-wedding custom column (Part two), and it shows the shape: a title, a body, a place in the run, a ceiling, and malformed input dropped.

The build: a couple-authored section on the main page, in any phase — a photo or snippet plus a heading and a paragraph, using the same four chapter arrangements as the story. Pro, because adding a section is arranging, not fixing a word we wrote. The shipped set stays closed (editorial-order.ts builds an exhaustive record so the compiler can prove no section is missing); a custom section is a new keyed type, not a hole in that record.

Recommendation, and one question for the owner: keep a ceiling, six per phase — a page of forty blocks is unreadable on a phone. Whether the existing 6 / 80 / 4,000 applies unchanged is his call (Part five).

Build 5

Google Drive as a source

Every exported Drive function writes: pushToDriveCopy, uploadFileToDrive, createDriveFolder, ensureArtifactFolder. Nothing reads a file back out. Picking a photo from Drive needs Google’s own picker — a new integration, not an extension. Not blocking: uploads work without it, and the editor’s fourth media door already says Not built.

Re-measure: git grep -n "^export async function" apps/web/lib/drive-copy.ts apps/web/lib/drive-upload.ts.

Build 6 · the large onePro

The canvas editor

Needs JavaScript — drag, handles, undo, z-order — and that is fine: it is the dashboard, not the guest page. The prototypes fix the contract: per section, a JSON of arrangement (one of four, canvas sections only), the motion preset and any Fine-tune overrides (How it runs, In, During, Out, Stagger, Duration, Delay — each absent when Auto), the focal point (1–9), and the media reference. The guest page renders that JSON with CSS; the editor is the only script.

Build it against the story section first, not the hero: the story is the hard one (a photo and words that must stack), and everything it solves the hero inherits. The form middle for the eleven filled-in widgets is the same shell with the canvas swapped for an outline and a Content tab — build it second, on the schedule, and it is mostly the fields that already exist.

Build 8 · part-provenPro

A page mode: the page moves, or the scenes hold

The owner spotted it: “if all has no background, they are just transitioning in and out as they scroll. looking like they are not scrolling down.” That is a page mode, not a side effect. Page moves is the ordinary long page: sections carry their own ground and travel up past the reader. Scenes hold: every section is transparent and pinned; the page still scrolls but nothing travels — the screen stays put and its contents change, driven by the scroll. Same markup, same content, one switch. It costs a progress mark (a guest cannot feel how much page is left), makes skimming harder, and must fall back to the plain page under prefers-reduced-motion.

What is proven and what is not. The pinning works and each scene fades in and out. The scenes do not truly cross-fade: one finishes leaving before the next arrives, measured at zero of twenty scroll positions having two scenes both part-visible. Two fixes were tried — moving the timeline to the spacer, and overlapping the spacers — and neither closed it. It has not been tested on a real phone. Do not sell the cross-fade until it exists.

Prototype: prototypes/scenes-hold-vs-page-moves-2026-09-23.html (position: sticky + animation-timeline: view(), no script). Re-measure the overlap before touching the plan: count scroll positions at which two .inner elements both have 0 < opacity < 1.

Part five

Open owner questions

Stated plainly, not answered here. Nothing below is already settled in the decision record.

Owner

Are manual uploads themselves Pro?

Settled: Drive is not a gate, and a couple without Drive gets their photos stored compressed. Not settled: the Pro list names “Manual uploads”, and the rule puts “their own photos, snippets, films” on the Pro side. So does uploading a photo need Pro at all — or does a free couple upload and simply get the compressed copy, with Pro buying the Drive originals?

Owner

Which editor survives?

/dashboard/[eventId]/website/editor and /app/site-editor/[eventId]/ both exist. The canvas editor in Build 6 has to land in one of them.

Owner

The nav vocabulary

The owner’s words: Memories · Detail · Home. The shipped bar (as the approved hub prototype reads it): Home · Details · Story · Camera · Watch · Gallery · Me. Which set is the guest’s?

Owner

A tablet band for the editor?

Below 900px the editor takes the phone layout. An unfolded foldable or an iPad in portrait therefore gets bottom sheets when it has room for columns. Add a middle band (two columns, Animate as a sheet), or leave two layouts?

Owner

Five phases, or four?

The shipped model is four (Part one). The owner named five today: Save the Date, Invitation/RSVP, before event, on the date, after event. “Before event” has no shipped phase. Is it a new phase — a final run-up in the last days, distinct from RSVP — or another name for the RSVP window?

Owner

The custom-section ceiling

Custom columns are capped at 6 per recap, 80 characters of title, 4,000 of body. Does the same ceiling apply to custom sections on the main page? Recommendation: keep a ceiling, six per phase.

Owner

Scenes hold: is fade-out-then-fade-in enough?

The page mode pins and each scene fades, but the scenes do not overlap — one is gone before the next arrives (Build 8). Two fixes did not close it. Is that handoff acceptable as it stands, or must the scenes truly cross-fade before the mode ships at all? And is a mode that hides how much page is left worth its progress mark on a phone? Nothing is assumed either way until it has been seen on a real phone.
