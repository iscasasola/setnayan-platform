## 2026-09-02 · feat(papic): a guest takes her own photograph off the live wall — herself, and immediately

Owner ruling 2026-09-02 (`DECISION_LOG.md`), settling item 6, verbatim in shape: a guest controls **the photos she SHOT and the photos she is TAGGED in — both** — and nobody else's. On the wall, when her photo is posted she can un-post it. She does **not** get per-audience consent switches, and she does **not** get to remove other people's photos.

**The mechanism already shipped; the writer did not.** `papic_photos.wall_hidden_at` / `papic_guest_captures.wall_hidden_at` are documented in `20261104000959` as the *"transient wall-only kill switch (reversible)"*, distinct from `hidden_at` (*"durable gallery/recap suppression"*), and `wall_visible_photos` already refuses to project a row carrying one. The only writers were admin moderation and the couple's Papic console (`wall_retract` / `wall_unhide`, both gated on `event_members`). **The person in the photograph had none.** This is that writer and nothing else — no consent infrastructure was built.

**Why a request could not do this job.** `askToTakeMyPhotoDown` (shipped 2026-08-28) is the right answer to *"delete my likeness"*: the photograph is not hers, it may hold four other people, so it goes to a person. It is the wrong answer to a photograph **on a screen in the room she is standing in**, where the only useful latency is none. So the tile now carries three controls for three different wishes — *Not me* (drop the tag), *Take it down* (ask a person), *Take it off the wall* (stop the projector, now).

**The scope is the whole risk, and it is tested as behaviour.** `/[slug]` is a public page and a guest has no account, so the guest id comes from the signed session cookie and never from an argument; `lib/guest-wall-unpost.ts` then decides which photographs are hers — her guest-camera captures, photographs shot on **her own Limited roll seat** (her personal QR *is* that camera's credential), and photographs carrying a **live** `photo_tags` row for her (a `removed_at` tombstone is not a tag: pressing "Not me" gives up the control in the same breath). Every predicate is re-checked in TypeScript on the returned row rather than trusted to the `.eq()` filters that fetched it — which is also what makes the refusals provable. `lib/guest-wall-unpost.test.ts` runs 24 cases against a stub client that **ignores filters** and records every `update()`; each refusal asserts `writes.length === 0`, and five sabotages were confirmed RED.

**It is the wall, and only the wall.** `hidden_at` is never written — a guard iterates every column this module writes and fails on anything that is not `wall_hidden_at`. The photograph stays in the couple's album and in her own gallery.

**Reversible, and only her own pull.** New `wall_hidden_by_guest_id` on both capture tables records who pulled it, because `wall_hidden_at` alone cannot tell *"that is me and I don't want it up there"* from a coordinator hiding a blurry frame. She can put back what she pulled; the hosts' moderation refuses with its own sentence rather than a silence. `wall_retract` / `wall_unhide` are `CREATE OR REPLACE`d body-identically except that both now clear the provenance, so no row claims a guest is holding down a photograph she no longer is.

**What the tile says.** `getGuestLiveGallery` now carries a per-photo wall state; the tile shows a pulsing **On the wall** / **Off the wall** badge (top-left, `pointer-events-none` like the credit — the whole tile opens the lightbox) and the ≥44px button lives in the lightbox, where there is room for it. A **failed** wall read renders as ON and keeps the control offered: a privacy button that disappears because a secondary read broke is the failure-looks-like-emptiness defect this repo keeps paying for, and the un-post is safe and idempotent on a photograph that is not projecting.

⚠ **Two guests tagged in one photo can each pull it.** The owner weighed exactly this and accepted it — being able to remove a picture of yourself outranks the other guest's wish to keep it. It is affordable here because the pull is wall-only and reversible; it was *not* affordable for withdrawn consent, which is why `20271159728048` blurs and keeps instead.

🔑 **Open owner question, deliberately not decided in code:** the couple's per-tile **Restore** (`unhideWallTile` → `wall_unhide`) can still put back a photograph a guest pulled. Nothing here changes that, and the provenance column is what makes the choice expressible either way. Ruling 3 of 2026-08-17 let either side toggle FaceBlock freely with "the guest is told" as the counterweight; the same shape may or may not be wanted here.

Exposure baseline regenerated: two `col` lines, both mirroring the grants their sibling `wall_hidden_at` columns already carry. No new table, no new policy, no new principal.

SPEC IMPACT: None beyond the ruling already recorded — the 2026-09-02 `DECISION_LOG.md` row *"A GUEST CAN UN-POST HER OWN PHOTO"* is the decision, and this implements it as written. No SKU, price or product-surface change.
