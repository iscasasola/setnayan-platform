## 2026-07-27 · feat(vendor): inner / outer service radius — the vendor declares their reach, and free travel becomes a fact instead of a guess

Owner-specced 2026-07-27: *"they have inner radius. this radius must comply to give free transportation fee if within this radius. outer radius is the overall range."* · *"Per vendor. Vendor has their HQ and x km from their HQ means free transportation fee."*

**What existed before this.** Reach was ONE number and purely **tier-derived** (`tierCaps().serviceRadiusKm`: free 0 · verified/solo 20 · pro 50 · enterprise/custom 100) — no vendor-declared radius column existed anywhere in the schema. Transport was expressed by **distance-blind flags** (`vendor_services.transport_included` / `transport_flat_fee_php`, `vendor_package_items.transport_mode`, `event_vendors.transport_php`), so a vendor could only say "transport included ALWAYS" or "flat fee ALWAYS". They could not say *"free within 15 km, chargeable beyond"* — which is how PH suppliers actually price. And a Pro vendor who in reality only serves Metro Manila was ranked as comfortable at 45 km with no way to say otherwise.

**Two rings, on `vendor_profiles`, measured from the HQ pin.** Migration `20271013561924` adds nullable `inner_radius_km` + `outer_radius_km` (INTEGER, matching the neighbouring `radius_km` columns) with three CHECK constraints: non-negative on each, and a NULL-tolerant `inner_radius_km <= outer_radius_km`. **No new distance math and no new geocoding** — `apps/web/app/dashboard/[eventId]/vendors/page.tsx` already haversines `vendor_profiles.hq_latitude/hq_longitude` against the event's venue per candidate; inner/outer are two thresholds on a number that already existed. Per VENDOR, not per service (the owner settled the schema question `Explore_Replan_BUILD_SPEC_2026-07-27.md` §17.3 left open — the rings hang off the HQ).

**The tier cap is enforced TWICE, and the second one is the one that matters.** At **write time** `validateServiceRadiusPair` refuses an outer radius above `tierCaps(tier).serviceRadiusKm`, reading `tier_state` from the database inside the server action — never from the form, which is whatever the browser says it is. At **read time** `effectiveOuterRadiusKm(declared, tier) = min(declared ?? cap, cap)` re-clamps on every read, which is what handles **downgrade**: a vendor who declared 50 km on Pro and lapses to Verified reads 20 km forever, with no backfill job, no cron and no downgrade hook to forget to call. `effectiveInnerRadiusKm` clamps the inner ring to the *effective* outer for the same reason — a stale 30 km free ring under a 20 km effective outer would otherwise make every venue 20–30 km out read "free travel" while simultaneously being out of range.

The cap is deliberately **not** a DB CHECK. A tier-reading CHECK would have to read `tier_state` from the same row and would therefore **fire on downgrade** — a lapsed vendor's row would become un-updatable, and any unrelated write to it (a logo change, a name reveal) would start throwing. The database enforces only the invariants that hold regardless of subscription state.

**Vendor UI — two fields, in plain words, where coverage already lives.** `ServiceRadiusFields` renders directly under the existing `ReachMap` in My Shop → Business → Coverage reach (`app/vendor-dashboard/shop/page.tsx` · `BranchPanel`). That component's own docstring already said the radius was *"read-only here; a follow-up makes it vendor-settable up to the tier ceiling"* — this is that follow-up, in place, no new page and no new route. Labels are **"Free travel within"** and **"Furthest we'll travel"**, each with a sentence saying what it means to a couple; the words *inner* and *outer* appear nowhere a vendor can see them. The plan ceiling renders inline ("Your plan allows up to N km") and the panel's own copy shifts from "You cover about N km" to "Your plan reaches about N km" — because under this model that number stopped being a claim about the vendor and became the cap on what they may claim. The card hides entirely on a plan with no reach (free tier, cap 0) rather than showing inputs that can only fail. A blank is a valid, un-nagged state.

**Couple-facing — a three-state badge that only fires on a real declaration.** `resolveReachBadge` (pure, in `lib/vendor-service-radius.ts`) owns the whole precedence, so the bench card (`shortlist-categories.tsx`) and the quickview inspector (`vendor-quickview-inspector.tsx`) are literally the same decision and cannot drift:

- inside inner → **"No travel fee"**
- inner→outer → **"Travel fee applies"** (still in range — the struck-through pin is reserved for genuinely out-of-range)
- beyond outer → **"Outside their range"**
- **either ring undeclared, or the distance unknown → EXACTLY today's tier-derived `reachesVenue` badge, verbatim.** A blank is never a penalty and never a new claim. Both boundaries are inclusive: "free within 15 km" means 15 km is free.

No new query for any of it. The two columns ride in the vendors page's existing `Promise.all` off the same `marketplaceIds` list — but in their **own select**, deliberately, for deploy-order safety (`lib/std-video-gate.ts:116` states the rule): Vercel ships on merge and migrations apply on their own schedule, so folding a not-yet-migrated column into the `profRes` select would answer 42703 and null the WHOLE row, stripping every picked vendor of its name, tier and reach. Isolated, the worst case is an empty rings map — which reads as "nobody has declared", which is the correct state in that window. The shop page soft-probes the same way, matching its existing `registration_number_raw` probe.

**The ranking/scorer is deliberately untouched.** `lib/compat-score.ts` and `lib/bench-sort.ts` are unchanged in this PR. §17.3's ranking hookup (outer radius as the distance decay scale, estimated travel fee feeding `budgetFit`) is a follow-up for once real declarations exist — wiring a scorer to a column that is 100% NULL in production would only move noise around.
