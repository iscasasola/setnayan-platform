In plain language

For someone who has not followed the thread.

What changes

  • The order. Credits first, then Coverage, then Allotment, then the room, then what you got.
  • The recommendation moves onto the page and shows its arithmetic: 146 guests × 150 a head. It already existed — it was only on the home tile.
  • Per-event-type numbers. Today a christening, a debut and a wedding are all quoted 150 a head. All 17 types get their own, admin-editable.
  • A minimum per guest, beside the maximum that already ships — checked against the pot.
  • Dedicated per-camera credits are retired. Every camera shoots the shared pool.
  • All 631 challenges become reachable, in 12 categories, with search and a count of how many celebrations picked each.
  • Blur becomes per person, with face tagging as a hard precondition.

What is deliberately NOT changing

  • The capture-window picker and its rules — another session owns that file. Only this block’s name and position move.
  • The QR render, the monogram resolver, the credit stepper, the filter looks. They ship and they work.
  • paparazzi_seats — 24 live rows.
  • Wedding stays at 150 a head, so no live couple’s number moves. 9 of the 11 live events are weddings — that is the acceptance test.

What this cannot prove

  • That it renders in the real app. This is hand-written HTML, not the React components. It proves the plan, not the build.
  • Anything about a real wedding. 26 captures, 0 allotments, 0 blurred faces in production.
  • The blur itself. Whether a detected face can be reliably tied to a tagged guest needs real photos on a real device. The circles here are a diagram.
  • I have not seen this rendered. The preview here shows local files as a static snapshot and refuses page tools, so there are no screenshots at phone and desktop width. I verified it by reading the file instead: zero <script> tags, and every panel non-empty.

Open questions

  1. The “you’ve used your 25 shots” wording. The one screen with no shipped words to borrow.
    Recommendation: the wording drawn above.
    Cost of getting it wrong: a guest reads “run out of shots”, believes the celebration is empty, and comes to you mid-reception.
  2. Does the learning loop ship dormant, or not at all this wave? 9 weddings hold 100,362 credits granted and one used.
    Recommendation: build it dormant, with the minimum-sample gate and the exhausted-pool exclusion baked in while the reasoning is fresh.
    Cost of getting it wrong: a later session writes the naive average, it reads ~0 per head, and the recommendation collapses to nothing.
  3. Is blur worth this wave at all? 146 guests, 0 have ever used it.
    Recommendation: keep it last and drop it without hesitation if the wave shrinks.
    Cost of getting it wrong: the biggest and most privacy-sensitive build delays four finished ones.
