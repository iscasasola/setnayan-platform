## 2026-08-08 · feat(realstories, blog): a "From the Journal" rail on Real Stories, and a storyteller's chapter that can sit inside an article — and quietly leave it

Warm Editorial Archive port, group "Real Stories hub + Journal article" (FABLE Public Marketplace spec § 3.3 / § 3.4, states § 4.B item 6 + § 4.C).

**What a person experiences.**

1. **Real Stories now ends with three planning guides.** After the wall of other people's stories and before the "your story could be next" panel, a short labelled rail — *From the Journal* — offers three of our guides. Somebody who has just spent five minutes reading about other people's weddings gets an obvious next step that is useful rather than a sign-up button. The rail is **absent entirely** until at least two guides have actually gone live; a rail of one looks broken, and an empty heading looks abandoned.

2. **A Journal article can now show a real storyteller's film where the writing mentions it.** A gold-framed card sits in the flow of the prose: the player, the chapter's name, who made it, which platform it plays on, and a link to their own page. **If that storyteller later makes their profile private, or unpublishes the chapter, the card simply is not there** — the paragraphs either side close up and the article still reads correctly. No broken player, no "this video is unavailable", no apology box on a marketing page. Somebody who took their story down does not leave a hole behind them.

**Honesty gates, stated.**

- *The rail:* there is no database read at all — the guide list is an in-code constant — so there is deliberately **no try/catch and no "coming soon" state**. Inventing one would manufacture a failure that cannot happen and would make the single real zero (every guide still future-dated) read as an outage. The only zero is silence.
- *The chapter card:* a failed read and a genuinely-withdrawn chapter **look identical on the page, on purpose** — showing nothing is honest either way, and an error box about someone else's video would be worse. They are no longer identical **in the logs**: `fetchPublishedChapterForShare` previously destructured only `{ data }` and dropped `{ error }` on both of its reads. Supabase resolves with `{ data: null, error }` rather than throwing, so a rejected query — an RLS denial, a renamed column, a network blip — produced exactly the same `null` as a legitimate absence, with green CI, forever. Both reads now log.
- Chapter resolution uses `Promise.allSettled`, so one unresolvable id can never blank the other chapters in the same article, and it can never take the article down with it.

**Traps hit.**

- **Placement was the whole bug surface on the rail.** The two shelves on Real Stories live inside a `searchMode` ternary that flips once the story pool crosses 50. Dropping the rail into that else-branch — the obvious place, right under the shelves — would have made it **silently disappear the day the page succeeds**. It is mounted after the ternary instead, with a comment saying why.
- **The spec's serif premise is falsified by the code.** § 3.3 asks for a Cormorant headline, "the Journal's serif travelling with Journal content". The Journal does not render Cormorant today — `--font-display` is Hanken Grotesk, and Cormorant only reaches a page through the `.sn-editorial` scope, which is not applied to `/blog`. So the rail renders the **actual shipped Journal card**, extracted out of the `/blog` index into `app/blog/_components/story-card.tsx` and imported by both. The serif question is a live one for the owner; forking a lookalike card to answer it would have created the second copy that drifts.
- **That extraction was the riskiest change here, not the new work.** `/blog` is a shipped public page. The move was verified **character-for-character** against `git show HEAD` before anything else ran, and `lint-port-no-lost-controls.mjs` confirms `/blog` lost no destination (the guard reads the whole route folder, so a sibling `_components/` still counts as the route's).
- **A mistyped chapter id is an invisible no-op** — hand-typed into an in-code constant, and a wrong one renders nothing forever, indistinguishable from a chapter that was withdrawn. `lib/blog-chapter-block.test.ts` now turns that into a CI failure. It was **sabotage-checked**: a bad id was injected into a real article and the test went red naming the article and block index, then reverted. It also pins that a chapter block leaks **no identity** into `articleBody` or the meta description — `/blog/[slug]` is indexed while a chapter's own page is `noindex`, so folding the storyteller's name or title into the JSON-LD would publish them somewhere they never agreed to. That is the kind of thing a later "improvement" reaches for, so it is a test rather than a comment.
- **`embed_provider` is nullable in the schema.** Unguarded, the byline would read "plays on undefined" on a public page.
- **`embed_url` is typed nullable on `PublicChapter`** even though the resolver only returns rows that carry one — checked rather than `!`-asserted, because the assertion becomes a lie the day the resolver is refactored.
- **Radius:** `lint-radius.mjs` forbids the spec's literal `rounded-[16px]`; the legal token above the embed frame's own 20px is `rounded-2xl` (22), so the frame does not read inverted.
- **CSP verified, not assumed** — `frame-src` already lists exactly the three hosts `normalizeEmbed` produces (`www.youtube-nocookie.com`, `www.instagram.com`, `www.tiktok.com`). A blocked iframe is another invisible absence; no change was needed, but it was checked rather than trusted.

- **The tests went green while the typechecker was red.** `tsx --test` is not a typechecker: under `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, indexing the parallel `chapterIds[i]` array yields `string | undefined`, and `tsc` refused it — at runtime it had been working perfectly. Fixed by reading the id once and checking it, rather than asserting a pairing the type system cannot see. Worth remembering that a green test run says nothing about whether CI's `tsc` will pass.
- **A wrapped command reports the wrapper's exit code, not the command's.** The first "clean" typecheck was a `pkill` I had issued (`exit 143`), and the run before that was a harness timeout (`exit 144`) — both surfaced as *completed*. Only the captured `$?` told the truth, and the one genuine failure was found on the run where I read it. Nothing here was ever reported clean on a red run, but it came close twice.

**Deliberately NOT done.** The rail does not enter the `/realstories` JSON-LD `ItemList` (that list is editorial-only; listing guides there would advertise them as real stories). `ViewBeacon` is **not** mounted on the article card — it would inflate a storyteller's public view count from a page that is not their chapter's page, and would put a client component with a write into a statically-rendered page. Nothing was widened: the block reuses the shipped `fetchPublishedChapterForShare` resolver and the shipped sandboxed `ChapterEmbedFrame` rather than adding a second of either.

**⚠ For the owner — one thing genuinely new, worth a look before the first chapter block is authored.** A chapter's own page is deliberately hidden from Google; a Journal article is not. So embedding a chapter puts a real storyteller's name and their chapter's title onto a searchable page for the first time. Their having published publicly is a reasonable stand-in for consent, but it is not the same consent. Keeping their identity out of the structured data (above) is the free half of the mitigation; whether to ask them first is a product call, not a code one. Related: the article implicitly stands behind whatever that channel later swaps in behind the same embed id — an editorial/moderation exposure, not a technical one.

Verification, exact: `tsc --noEmit` exit 0, zero errors · all 20 `apps/web/scripts/lint-*.mjs` guards pass · unit suite **7120/7120** · db suite **853/853**. New file `lib/blog-chapter-block.test.ts` (3 tests), both of its guarding assertions sabotage-verified — injected a bad id into a real article and it went red naming the article and block index; made `blogPlainText` leak the id and the privacy test went red — both reverted and re-run green. No migration, no new table, no new column; every column named was checked against `supabase/migrations/` first.
