# MB12 — Per-part finalization handshake

**Goal:** a part the vendor has agreed to build stops moving.

**Model:** Opus · high effort — a two-party state machine binding suppliers and couples. The
repo's one prior handshake needed an RPC repair this very week.
**Size:** 1.5 days. **Depends on:** MB2 (part registry), MB5 (the derivation-stop mechanism),
MB10 (category map), **and the owner's inheritance decision.**

## 🛑 Owner decides: does finalization inherit "a booking outranks any marker"?

**Recommendation: no.** `lockRequestStateOf` returning `locked` for any confirmed booking makes
sense for *bookings*. Auto-finalizing a **design part the vendor never reviewed** would fabricate
the very agreement the handshake exists to capture.

## Delivers

- A per-`(event, part, vendor)` finalization table **reusing `lib/lock-request-state.ts`'s
  vocabulary** — its states, its 48h `LOCK_ANSWER_WINDOW_HOURS`, its fuse labelling — as a new
  scope. **Never a second mechanism.** Two mechanisms that disagree about one fact each pass their
  own suite.
- The part → category map, shared with MB10
- Couple finalize / re-open in 02 and 03
- Vendor agree / decline in the vendor dashboard
- Notifications on request, agree, and re-open — **and the email allowlist entry.** The
  notification and the allowlist are two halves of one mechanism; having one is indistinguishable
  from having neither.
- **No finalize without a booked vendor in category** — and the UI shows what it is waiting for,
  rather than a dead button
- Re-open only by counter-handshake
- **A finalized part stops re-deriving from 00** — reuse MB5's `touchedRoles` mechanism

## Verify

- full db replay + Ugat (new subsystem → add a `UGAT_TYPES` node)
- `pnpm exec tsc --noEmit` from `apps/web`
- state-machine unit tests covering **every** transition including expiry
- guard: a finalized part never re-derives — sabotage: re-derive it, confirm red
- guard: finalize with no booked vendor is impossible **at the action**, not merely hidden in the UI

## Owner decides first

The booking-inheritance question.
