## 2026-08-29 · fix(stories): the "feature our story" switch reaches every kind of day, and both screens stop claiming a blocked celebration is eligible

**Found on a real celebration.** The owner published the story for *Movie Night* — a `date` event on 20 Aug — and asked why it never appeared on the Setnayan home page. Four things had to be true and none of them were; two of them were ours, not his.

**1 · The opt-in switch was wedding-only, two weeks after the gallery stopped being.** On 2026-08-15 the owner ruled that every kind of celebration may be written up (*"each event they create will have an editorial not just wedding"*), naming `date` and `hangout` out loud (*"making it public will be the user's decision … so yes"*). Five `event_type='wedding'` filters came out of `lib/showcase-db.ts`, the weddings sitemap and the privacy-page panel. **Two doors did not move:** `editorial-editor.tsx` rendered the *"Feature our story in Stories"* switch behind an `isWedding` boolean computed at its call site, and `setStoryShowcase` refused a non-wedding opt-in **server-side**, quoting a filter that no longer existed. So fifteen of the sixteen kinds could write a story, publish it, and were never shown the one control that lets anyone see it.

🔑 **A gate whose handle was removed is invisible from both sides** — the gallery looks correctly empty, and the couple looks like somebody who simply never opted in. The kind question now has exactly one home (`editorialAllowsEventType`), which both doors ask; a newly added celebration type inherits the answer instead of waiting for somebody to remember these two files. The opt-in also fails **closed** on an unreadable event — an unknown kind is not a consented one.

**2 · The one caveat that did render named the wrong remedy.** It said *"Make it Public or **Unlisted**"* — while the gallery's read was tightened the same 2026-08-15 from `!= 'private'` to `= 'public'`, precisely because "unlisted" is what the privacy screen sells as **link only**. Following our own instruction left you invisible, silently. 🔑 **A caveat that names the wrong remedy is worse than no caveat: it gets followed.** It also only rendered while the switch was already ON, so the person still deciding was told nothing; the caveat now depends on the page's visibility alone, and names the setting they actually chose (`invited_accounts` was a real fourth state missing from the `LandingVisibility` union, so it used to print "Private" at somebody who had picked tagged-accounts-only).

**3 · "On — eligible to be featured" was a claim the privacy page could not keep**, and it contradicted a choice made higher up the same screen. Saying yes there records **consent**; the gallery also needs the page itself to be Public and to have an address. Somebody who set Private above and switched Stories on below got a green tick and waited. The badge now says which of the two is missing, in the words of the control that fixes it. **Consent is not eligibility.**

**Not changed, deliberately.** The 30-day post-event grace window and the front door's "two published stories before the grid replaces the written invitation" threshold (`STORIES_MIN_PUBLISHED`) are both working as designed — they are why *Movie Night* would still wait until 19 September and would appear on `/realstories` before it appears on `/`. Consent stays a per-USER flag; making it per-event is a migration and its own change.

**Guard:** `lib/stories-opt-in-is-reachable.test.ts` — 5 assertions, **7 mutations, every one printed before → after and every one RED**: restoring the hardcoded `!== 'wedding'` refusal, failing open on an unread event, re-deriving the switch from a wedding boolean, handing the editor a boolean from the call site, re-advising "Unlisted", hiding the caveat behind the switch, and dropping the blocker test from the privacy badge. 🪤 Its first cut **cried wolf**: scanning the privacy page whole, it went red on *"Choose Public or Unlisted to let them in"* — a correct sentence about guests reaching a launched Save-the-Date. The scan is cut to the Stories panel now; a guard that cries wolf teaches you to skim past the one time it is right.
