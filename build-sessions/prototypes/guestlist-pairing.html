1 · Where pairing lives

On the guest row (guests.pair_with_guest_id, mutual, written by pair_guests()). The "who walks with whom" view is the Walking order panel already shipped in PR #5759 — it reads the same rows inside a role view. No second table, no second screen.

2 · Two rows, linked — not one

RSVP, meal, seat and contact are facts about a person: a ninang can decline while her ninong attends. So the roster never removes a column. The pair collapses to one line only where the pair is the unit: the walking order and the print.

3 · Walking order ≠ table

Two orderings, two columns: entourage_order (per line, both halves equal) and event_seat_assignments (per chair). The Seat column stays per person on the roster; the walking panel shows no seat. Moving a pair up never moves a chair.

4 · A half who is "not a guest"

Everyone who walks is a row. A ceremony-only sponsor keeps invited_to_blocks = [ceremony], prints normally, and the roster says ceremony only with no seat. A child bearer pairs with a teen escort like anyone else. A +1 name string cannot walk — promote them to a row first.
